Servers nobody stopped

Dev servers and watchers keep running after the agent that started them is done, holding ports, memory and battery.

Copies filling your disk

Agents make a fresh copy of your project for each task. Each one carries its own dependencies and builds, often gigabytes, and few get removed.

PollymetricEvery copy, the space it holds, when it was last touched, and a safe way to remove it.

Memory you can't account for

A local model keeps gigabytes in memory for as long as it's loaded, even while it sits idle. Your Mac just calls it "ollama".

Three simple steps to get started

No account required. Choose what to enable during setup.

1

Download and drag to Applications

Open Pollymetric.dmg and drag Pollymetric into Applications.

2

Open it and choose what to enable

Choose whether Pollymetric starts at login and which optional tools to enable. Connect an AI agent if you want help investigating problems.

Nothing needs youIt'll flag anything worth a look
3

Get on with your day

It lives in your menu bar, gray while all is well, and speaks up only when something needs you.

The first time you open it: this early release isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS will say it can't verify the developer. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Notarized releases that skip this step are on the way.

Why "Pollymetric"

Polly, as in the parrot. Metric, as in measure.

AI gets compared to a parrot. Pollymetric keeps count of what the parrots are doing to your machine. Free, open source and MIT licensed.
